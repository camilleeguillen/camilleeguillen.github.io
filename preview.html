Forest loss and urban growth along Colorado's Front Range, 2001–2021

Being presented at the AGU Fall Meeting, December 2026

How has forest cover changed along the Front Range's elevation gradient over 20 years, and how much of that change is linked to urbanization?

Working on a team with Xinran Zhou and Malia Goldstien, mentored by Dr. Sandra Durán and Dr. Mohammad Saif, I compared NLCD land cover from 2001 and 2021 with elevation, Landsat imagery, and MODIS burn data. We processed and analyzed everything in Python and GIS.

CSU Extension  /  June 2026 to present

Colorado farmers market price reports

Each week I track fruit, vegetable, meat, and egg prices at farmers markets across Colorado, including Boulder, Fort Collins, Montrose, Palisade, and Pueblo. In R, I calculate the lowest, highest, and average price for each product and put together 5–10 reports that CSU Extension publishes for growers.

Farmers use the reports to compare prices between markets and set fair prices for their farm stands and CSA shares.

    Equitable Agriculture and Environmental Management Program  /  Santa Barbara  /  July 2025

    Does heavy traffic mean more polluted water?

    Tire wear sheds tiny particles that can wash into streams and lakes. In a two-week intensive program, my team tested whether Southern California census tracts with more traffic also have more impaired water bodies, using CalEnviroScreen 4.0 environmental data for more than 1,600 tracts.

    In R, we ran an ANOVA and a regression that controlled for county. Traffic had a statistically significant but very small effect, while differences between counties explained far more of the variation. We also found that a single extreme tract in San Diego made the relationship look much stronger than it is. We presented policy-focused recommendations to faculty and peers.

    GIS lab portfolio

    A selection of my lab work in ArcGIS Pro, from classifying satellite imagery to modeling terrain and automating a suitability analysis.

    I classified a high-resolution image of a mountain stream valley into land cover classes two ways: unsupervised, where the software groups similar pixels on its own, and supervised, where I drew training polygons for classes like water and grass. The supervised version was more accurate because I could control the classes, while the unsupervised one was faster but harder to interpret.

    Starting from a digital elevation model near Red Feather Lakes, Colorado, I derived elevation classes, slope, 20 m contour lines, and a hillshade to show the shape of the land.

    To find suitable land within a watershed, I buffered roads and streams, selected the right land cover types, and combined the layers with intersect and erase tools. I automated every step in ModelBuilder so the whole analysis can be rerun in one click.

    I georeferenced aerial imagery of Fort Collins using control points, then digitized parks, a golf course, waterways, and local businesses as polygons, lines, and points.
